Introduction

The DEP Annual Survey 2026 reached 1,732 respondents across every stage of the Philippine data career — students finishing degrees, job seekers waiting for their first break, career shifters pivoting from other fields, and professionals pushing for the next level.

This report organises the significant findings into a single career journey narrative, written for four audiences: those trying to break in, those making a pivot, those climbing further, and the community leaders trying to build something that helps all of them.

Methodology: Every figure cited passed a pairwise z-test at 95% confidence. Cells with n < 30 are suppressed. These are not averages — they are signals.
Part 1
For Students & Job Seekers
Breaking In: Roles, Industries, Job Hunting, and Salary
"What are the data roles out there? What will increase my odds of getting hired? What salary ranges should I expect?"

The Three Roles You Are Competing For

The dominant employed roles are Data Analysis & Insights (DA), Data Engineering (DE), and Software Engineering (SE). Each has a distinct profile worth understanding before you apply.

DA · Data Analysis & Insights
41.1%
of DA practitioners work at a multinational employer — significantly higher than DE or SE. DA is the multinational's workhorse.
  • Skews female: 26.6% of DA practitioners are female vs. lower in DE
  • Heavily full-time: 79.9% employed full-time, significantly higher than career shifters
  • Second-largest employer: Banking/Finance/Insurance at 18.1%, significantly above SE
DE · Data Engineering
90.0%
of DE practitioners are full-time — the highest full-time rate of any role. If you want DE, aim for full-time positions, not contracts.
  • Male-dominated: 14.1% of male respondents are in DE vs. lower for females
  • Concentrates at foreign and multinational employers in the higher salary bands
SE · Software Engineering
35.7%
of SE practitioners had a side gig in the past 12 months — significantly higher than DA (20.6%) and DE (18.8%). SE is the most side-gig-friendly role.
  • Highest freelance rate among the three roles
  • Highest daily AI usage for work: 70.4%, significantly higher than DA

Which Industries Have the Most Jobs?

44.0%
of multinational-employed respondents work in IT/BPO/Business Services — significantly higher than local employers. This is the dominant sector.

Banking, Finance & Insurance is the second-strongest sector — 18.1% for DA roles and 18.7% for hybrid workers, both significantly higher than their comparators.

Early-Entry Sectors for 20–24 Year Olds
  • Government: 8.0% of 20–24-year-olds, significantly higher than mid-career groups. Active early-entry pipeline, especially for onsite roles.
  • Manufacturing: 8.8% of 20–24-year-olds and 6.6% of career shifters — both significantly higher than the 25–34 age bands.
  • Research: 5.6% of 20–24-year-olds and 5.7% of PT/Contract/Freelance workers. Academic and research institutions hire early.

How People Actually Find Jobs

74.6%
of professionals use LinkedIn — significantly higher than career shifters. Among DE practitioners it reaches 80.5%. LinkedIn is not optional.
47.4%
of 20–24-year-olds use Indeed — significantly higher than older age groups. The primary complement to LinkedIn at entry level.
  • Jobstreet: 42.7% for local employers and 35.5% for multinationals — both significantly higher than foreign employers. Use it for domestic companies.
For Foreign / Remote Work
  • OnlineJobs.ph: 15.1% for foreign-employer respondents — significantly elevated. The gateway to foreign remote roles.
  • Upwork: 10.3% among PT/Contract/Freelance — the freelance platform path to foreign engagements.
  • Referrals: 16.4% among PT/Contract/Freelance workers. Your network matters most when working independently.

What Credentials Actually Matter?

A Bachelor's degree is the minimum bar — 88.4% of professionals and 89.2% of job seekers hold one, both significantly higher than students still studying.

CS vs Non-CS Backgrounds
  • BS Computer Science dominates DE, SE, and students (37.3%). Males show significantly higher CS degree rates than females.
  • BS ECE is significantly more common among career shifters (24.4% vs. professionals) — frequent launch pad for the data pivot.
  • Non-CS degree: 58.4% of career shifters have no CS or data degree — significantly more than any other group. It is the norm, not the exception.
Degree Requirement by Role
Key signal: SE practitioners have an 83.9% comp/data degree rate — the highest of any role, significantly above DA (52.8%). For software-adjacent data work, the CS degree is nearly a prerequisite. For DA, it is less critical.
  • BS Applied Mathematics / Statistics: Significantly higher in DA (13.5% and 5.8% respectively). The analytics-heavy credential path.

What Salary Should You Expect?

35.5%
of 20–24-year-olds land in the ₱15k–₱25k band — the single largest salary bucket for this age group, significantly higher than every older cohort.
25.5%
earn ₱25k–₱35k. Most fresh entrants land between ₱15k and ₱35k gross.
Employer Type Impact

Local employers concentrate at lower bands (₱15k–₱25k at 25.8%, ₱25k–₱35k at 25.1% — both significantly higher than multinationals). Targeting a multinational or foreign employer as your first employer is a salary strategy.

Tenure signal: 32.6% of 20–24-year-olds have been in their current salary band for 3 months or less — the highest of any age group. Early career moves fast. Do not anchor too hard on your first number.
Part 2
For Career Shifters
The Pivot: Skills, Hardware, Cloud, and Content
"What are the top skills I need for DA or DE? What hardware should I use? Should I have cloud projects in my portfolio? What about content creation?"

Career shifters are statistically distinct: more likely to be 25–39, working onsite (52.7%), in lower salary bands, with a non-CS degree (58.4%), and carrying admin, engineering, or customer service experience. Their satisfaction scores are significantly lower than professionals at every band from 1–5. The pivot period is genuinely hard. The data below is meant to shorten it.

Core Skills: What DA and DE Practitioners Actually Use

DA Track — Data Analysis & Insights
  • SQL — non-negotiable first skill. Professionals (47.3%), DE (55.7%) all show significantly elevated usage. Start here.
  • MS Excel — 64.6% of DA practitioners use it for transformation, significantly higher than DE. Career shifters already using Excel are partway in.
  • Power BI — 42.4% DA usage, significantly higher than DE and SE. Power BI is to DA what Airflow is to DE.
  • DAX — 29.2% of DA practitioners, significantly higher than DE/SE. Mandatory if Power BI is your target.
  • MS Power Query (37.5%), Power Automate (15.6%), Power BI Dataflows (21.4%) — all significantly higher for DA. The Microsoft Power Platform stack is the DA lane in the Philippine market.
  • Python — 36.9% of professionals, significantly higher than career shifters. Add Python after Excel and SQL, not before.
DE Track — Data Engineering
  • Python — 51.9% of DE practitioners use it for transformation, significantly higher than DA. Not optional.
  • SQL — 55.7% of DE practitioners, the highest of any role for ingestion/transformation.
  • Airflow — 42.9% of DE practitioners for orchestration — one of the largest role-specific gaps in the entire survey. Airflow proficiency is a credible DE signal.
  • dbt — 27.3% of DE practitioners, significantly higher than DA/SE. Learn alongside SQL.
  • Databricks — 22.8% ingestion, 28.8% warehousing for DE, both significantly higher than other roles.
  • Snowflake — 42.4% in warehousing, significantly higher for DE than any other role. A legitimate portfolio anchor.
  • Azure Data Factory, AWS Glue, Fivetran, PySpark — all show significantly higher DE usage. Pick one cloud pipeline tool as your portfolio focus.

Should You Have Cloud Projects in Your Portfolio?

Yes — and the data is specific about which clouds.

53.6%
of DE practitioners use AWS — significantly higher than DA. AWS is the DE cloud.
50.7%
of DE practitioners also use Azure. Many use both. Choose one, get proficient, then build toward the second.
GCP as Starting Point

GCP shows up strongly among career shifters (59.0%, significantly higher than professionals) and PT/Contract/Freelance workers (69.2%). Its accessible free tier makes it a credible first cloud project. Supplement with AWS or Azure to match where employers invest.

Certification as proof: AWS Cloud Certification is significantly more common among professionals (9.4%) and DE/SE practitioners vs. DA. A certification fills the experience gap on a resume when years of DE employment are not yet available.

What Hardware Should You Use?

  • Laptop: 89.1% of female respondents and 89.2% of professionals use one as their primary device. For hybrid work (90.9%), laptops dominate. This is the baseline.
  • Desktop: 40.7% of career shifters use a desktop, significantly higher than students. If onsite, a desktop environment is normal.
  • Virtual Machine (VM): 20.0% of DE practitioners use a VM, significantly higher than DA/SE. Extends hardware for cloud-like local environments.
  • Workstation: 11.4% of professionals — not entry-level, but the environment you are heading toward.

What About Content Creation?

9.3% of career shifters do Content as part of their non-data activities — significantly higher than professionals. PT/Contract/Freelance workers also show elevated content involvement (14.6%).

This is a real bridge strategy: if your current role involves content, marketing, or communications, layering in data analytics for content performance, campaign tracking, or audience segmentation is a credible and portfolio-visible pivot path.

Tool signal: Notion (18.3% career shifters), WordPress (5.7%), and HubSpot are significantly elevated in adjacent-to-data roles. If you are already using these, that toolset is recognisable in the community.
Part 3
For Mid-Career Professionals
Going Up: Salary, Satisfaction, and Staying Sharp
"How do I move to the next salary band? Is a side gig common? How long should I stay before looking for more? Is a Master's worth it? What drives job satisfaction? Are my skills updated?"

The Salary Ladder: What the Data Shows

15.0%
of 30–34-year-olds earn ₱125k–₱250k — significantly higher than every other age group. This is the professional breakthrough band.
3.7%
of professionals earn ₱250k+ — significantly higher than career shifters. Senior compensation is in a separate tier.
Employer Type — The Strongest Lever

Multinational employers are significantly overrepresented in the ₱45k–₱125k bands. Foreign employers are significantly stronger at ₱125k–₱250k (11.7% vs. 4.3% local). Moving from a local to a multinational or foreign employer is the single most impactful structural career move for salary growth in this dataset.

The remote premium: Remote workers show significantly higher concentrations across every salary band above ₱55k. Onsite concentration is heaviest at the lower bands. Positioning for remote-eligible roles is a salary strategy, not just a lifestyle preference.

How Long Should You Stay Before Moving?

1–2 yrs
Most common tenure window for 25–29 (30.6%) and 30–34 (31.1%) year-olds — both significantly higher than the 20–24 cohort. This is the modal move window in the core professional age bands.
  • 2–5 years: Significantly more common among 35–39-year-olds (33.3%). Senior professionals stay longer before moving.
  • 6 months–1 year: Significantly higher for full-time professionals (22.1%). The first move often comes within the year.

Is a Side Gig Common?

35.7%
of SE practitioners had a side gig in the past 12 months — significantly higher than DA (20.6%) and DE (18.8%). SE is the most side-gig-active role.
44.1%
of PT/Contract/Freelance workers report a side gig — significantly higher than full-time employees.

Remote workers show significantly elevated side-gig rates (29.5%) vs. onsite (18.2%) and hybrid (19.4%) workers. The flexibility of remote work enables supplemental income.

For full-time DA/DE: 79–81% report no side gig. The full-time data role tends to be all-in. If supplemental income is the goal, SE skills and remote positioning create more room.

Is a Master's Degree Worth It?

10.1%
of DA practitioners hold a Master's degree — significantly higher than DE and SE. The Master's pays off more in DA than in engineering-track roles.
Prevalence by Age
  • 4.1% at 25–29 → 13.1% at 30–34 → 16.5% at 35–39 — each step significantly higher than the previous. Accumulated progressively, not front-loaded.
  • Multinational (9.4%) and foreign (8.6%) employers show significantly higher Master's rates vs. local (4.4%). A meaningful differentiator for large multinationals.
Bottom line: For DA targeting a senior multinational role, a Master's in Applied Mathematics, Data Science, or Statistics is a credible investment. For DE, there is no significant Master's signal — certifications and hands-on project depth deliver more return.

What Is Driving Job Satisfaction?

Score 10
Significantly more common among professionals (10.2%), foreign-employed (13.5%), remote workers (12.4%), and PT/Contract/Freelance (12.6%) — all vs. career shifters.
Who is most satisfied? Foreign-employed remote workers. Flexibility + employer calibre + compensation is the satisfaction formula.
Work Setup Impact

Onsite workers show significantly elevated rates at scores 1, 2, and 5. Hybrid workers outperform onsite on scores 6, 8, 9, and 10. Hybrid is the satisfaction sweet spot for full-time employed professionals.

For shifters: Significantly higher rates at every low satisfaction score (1–5). The pivot period is the dissatisfaction period. It passes — but the data confirms it is real.

Are My Skills Updated? The Professional Tool Stack

Collaboration & Workflow
  • Microsoft Teams (58.6%), SharePoint (37.9%), Confluence (17.8%), Jira (27.3%): All significantly higher for professionals. Enterprise collaboration fluency is a senior-level expectation.
  • Power Automate (19.5%), PowerApps (16.1%), OneDrive (29.4% DA): Full Microsoft 365 Power Platform fluency is expected, especially in DA roles.
Learning Platforms Professionals Use
  • O'Reilly (5.9%), LinkedIn Learning (20.2%), MicrosoftLearn (14.1%): All significantly elevated for professionals vs. students/shifters.
  • Udemy (32.6%), Coursera: Both significantly higher for professionals and 30–34-year-olds. Self-paced structured learning is a professional habit.
AI Tool Adoption
The debate is over. AI adoption is no longer a discussion in this community — it's a fact. 65% of professionals use AI tools daily. The question has shifted from "should we use AI?" to "which tools, for what, and how fast?"
65.1%
of professionals use AI tools daily for work — significantly higher than students and career shifters. The professional group is already AI-native.
  • Copilot: 52.0% of professionals — significantly higher than career shifters. The enterprise AI of choice.
  • Claude: 30.5% of professionals — significantly higher than career shifters. A marker of more advanced and diverse AI usage.
  • GitHub Copilot: Elevated among DE (16.9%) and SE (29.6%), significantly above DA. The vibe coding crowd is already here — SE practitioners lead on both AI adoption and side gigs, the exact profile of someone shipping faster with AI assistance.
Part 4
For DEP Community Leaders
Engagement, Adjacent Platforms, and the Learning University
"What can we do to increase engagement? We partner with DataCamp — what else are we missing? What are members already using that we can build toward? Is a self-paced university viable?"

How the Community Currently Engages With DEP

92.3%
of students and 92.5% of job seekers report the DEP DataCamp scholarship — both significantly higher than professionals and career shifters. The scholarship owns the early-career audience.
Beyond the Scholarship
  • DEP Discord: 40.0% of professionals, 34.0% of career shifters — both significantly higher than students. Discord is the professional retention channel. Already working for the hardest segment to keep.
  • DEP DataMasters: 19.0% of professionals, 10.7% of career shifters — both significantly higher than students. Resonates with employed practitioners.
  • DEP Puso Project: 22.3% of professionals — significantly higher than students and career shifters. Strong flagship engagement.
  • DEP Meetup Group: 22.7% of professionals, 28.8% of DE practitioners — both significantly higher than students. In-person community is a professional-stage behaviour.
Worth noting: The scholarship reaches students and job seekers far better than any other DEP initiative. But many of these members access DEP primarily through a mobile phone — which makes Discord, meetups, and project-based learning much harder to join. Lower engagement with other initiatives likely reflects a device access barrier, not a lack of interest. Reaching this group more deeply starts with getting them a proper machine to work on.

DataCamp: Who Is Actually Using It?

  • Job seekers: 54.0% use DataCamp — significantly higher than students and career shifters.
  • Professionals: 55.7% — significantly higher than students and career shifters.

DataCamp is strongest among job seekers and professionals — people actively applying skills to work. The platform's value compounds at these stages, not at the student stage.

Gender gap: Males: 51.4% use DataCamp — significantly higher than females. Worth investigating whether content or marketing is a factor.

What Else Are They Using?

High-Usage Learning Platforms by Segment
  • Udemy: 32.6% of professionals and 35.4% of multinational employees — both significantly higher than students. The self-paced upskilling platform of the professional segment.
  • Coursera: 36.7% of 30–34-year-olds and 35.0% of career shifters — the career-pivot platform.
  • LinkedIn Learning: Professionals (20.2%), DA (21.1%), multinational (19.0%) — all significantly higher. Embedded in professional workflows.
YouTube — The Underrated Giant
68.7%
of students use YouTube for learning — significantly higher in its comparison. 67.0% of DA practitioners too. Still the primary free learning resource across career stages, yet underutilised by DEP as a curated channel.
  • GitHub (57–58%): Dominant among students and job seekers. The primary entry-level practice environment.
  • FreeCodeCamp (20.2% students, 18.8% job seekers): Both significantly higher than career shifters. Free curriculum with strong early-career adoption.
  • W3Schools (37.0% students): Still widely used for fundamentals.
Adjacent Communities
  • Data Analytics Philippines: 47.0% of professionals, 52.0% of DA practitioners — the largest adjacent community for the professional analytics segment.
  • Power BI Pilipinas: 30.9% professionals, 38.3% DA, 39.3% of 35–39-year-olds — deeply embedded in the senior analytics segment.
  • Analytics Association of the Philippines: 18.5% professionals, 21.1% multinational employees — the institutional body with corporate reach.
  • DEVCON Philippines: 18.5% students, 21.4% SE practitioners — bridges SE and data roles.
  • Community: SPARTA PH: 13.2% of 30–34-year-olds, 9.2% of career shifters — government upskilling resonates with the career-pivot audience.

The Self-Paced Learning University: Is It Viable?

87.2%
of job seekers cite Upskill/Skill Acquisition as a need — significantly higher than professionals. The demand is sharpest at the entry and pivot stages.
86.3%
of career shifters also cite Upskill/Skill Acquisition. Two of DEP's most important audiences are explicitly asking for this.
Format That Works
  • Udemy/Coursera-style self-paced: Both platforms show significantly elevated usage among the exact audiences DEP targets. The format is proven and familiar.
  • GitHub-anchored projects: Students use GitHub at 57–58% — already their primary project environment. Meeting students where they already work.
  • Discord event delivery: 16.7% of professionals and 10.7% of students attended DEP Discord events, both significantly higher than career shifters. Cohort-based live sessions via Discord are a viable delivery channel.
Framing matters: Career advancement is the #1 stated need across career shifters (81.9%) and onsite workers (82.4%) — both significantly higher than their comparators. A DEP learning initiative that leads with career advancement outcomes — not just skill acquisition — speaks to the most urgent expressed need in the survey.

Volunteer Capacity: Who Will Help Build It?

40–41%
of job seekers, professionals, and males say they are willing to share knowledge — all significantly higher than students alone. The community wants to teach.
  • Mentor: Significantly more common among 30–34 (12.7%), 35–39 (12.9%), and multinational employees (14.9%). Senior professionals at multinationals are the mentorship supply.
  • Teach specific skills: DE practitioners (12.2%) — significantly higher than DA/SE. DE is willing to teach hard technical content.
  • Share experiences: Job seekers (32.9%) and DE practitioners (33.8%) — both significantly higher than students/DA. The career-story format is where job seekers and DE want to contribute.
Bottom line: The instructors are already here. Senior professionals in their 30s at multinationals — especially in DE and DA — are willing to teach, mentor, and share. Build the learning program around them and the supply-demand match takes care of itself.
Already happening: Volunteer-led initiatives are already emerging organically from the community. A DEP member has launched a "vibecode camp" — a practical, AI-assisted coding program that reflects exactly where the community has moved. This is what volunteer capacity looks like in action: no top-down mandate, just a practitioner who saw a need and built something. The data shows more of this is possible.

The Access Problem: A Laptop Changes Everything

Most DEP learning initiatives assume a laptop. GitHub projects, cloud CLIs, Discord, online sessions — none of these work well on a phone. Yet many of DEP's early-career members, especially students and career shifters in under-served segments, are mobile-only.

40.7%
of career shifters use a desktop as their primary device — significantly higher than students. A fixed home setup, not a laptop, is the norm for many members trying to make the pivot.

A refurbished laptop drive — machines running Linux, pre-configured for Python, SQL, and cloud tools — directly removes the biggest barrier between a member and the rest of the DEP ecosystem. It is also the kind of initiative where the community's existing volunteer base can contribute: sourcing, refurbishing, and setting up machines does not require a budget, just hands.

The real unlock: The DataCamp scholarship gets members in the door. A working laptop keeps them there. Without it, the self-paced university, the GitHub projects, and the Discord community all stay out of reach for the segment that needs them most.
The Shape of a Data Career in the Philippines

The 1,732 respondents of the 2026 DEP Annual Survey trace a consistent arc.

It starts with a student at 20–24, likely studying CS or ECE, building on GitHub and YouTube, earning a DataCamp scholarship, and applying via LinkedIn and Indeed to local employers. They land between ₱15k and ₱35k and start moving within three to six months.

Running alongside that student is a career shifter — 25–39 years old, coming from engineering, admin, or education, learning SQL and Power BI via Coursera and Udemy, working onsite for a local employer, with satisfaction scores measurably lower than their professional peers. They are asking the same question every week: how do I get in? The data answers: SQL first, Power BI or Python next, a cloud project after that, LinkedIn and OnlineJobs.ph as the channels.

By 30–34, the professional who stayed the course is earning ₱45k–₱125k, working hybrid or remote for a multinational or foreign employer, using Teams and Jira alongside Databricks and Snowflake, adopting AI tools daily, and scoring 7–10 on satisfaction.

The community holding this arc together — DEP — is most deeply embedded at the scholarship end and the Discord end. The gap is the transition: from scholarship recipient to active community member, from first job to employed professional. That gap is exactly where a DEP learning initiative, built on the tools and formats this community already uses, and taught by the senior professionals who are already willing to teach, has the most to give.
